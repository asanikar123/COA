A sanctuary you can read. A home built of words.

A refreshed mark for the world's largest sanctuary for exiled and persecuted writers — drawn from the org's own House Poem, and made to scale from a favicon to a festival banner.

The idea

The House of Poems

In 2004, City of Asylum's first resident — the exiled Chinese poet Huang Xiang — painted his verse across the façade of a house on Sampsonia Way. That house, covered in poetry, became the org's most enduring image: proof that a silenced voice, given a home, fills it.

The mark is that house. Its walls are made of lines of a poem, ragged like a real stanza. Beneath them, a doorway stands open in City of Asylum red — the light left on, the welcome that defines the work.

Anatomy

Three ideas, one silhouette

  • A

    The gable — home & sanctuary

    A single, archetypal house: safety, permanence, the two-plus years of residency that set this program apart.

  • B

    The stanza — the writer's work

    Three ragged lines of verse fill the walls: the House Poem, and the full-length work every resident goes on to create.

  • C

    The open door — the welcome

    A doorway in signature red, always open. The one warm accent, and the emotional center of the mark.

Handling

Keep it honest

Do

  • Give it clear space equal to the width of the doorway on every side.
  • Keep the door City-of-Asylum red — it's the one fixed colour.
  • Use the charcoal mark on light grounds, the cream mark on dark.
  • Reach for the mark alone below ~28px or for avatars.

Don't

  • Recolour the stanza lines or fill the house with a photo.
  • Stretch, shear, or rotate the silhouette.
  • Set the wordmark in a different typeface, or make "of" upright.
  • Place the charcoal logo on a busy or dark photograph.
