About City of Asylum

A grassroots safe haven, built by community

Our Mission City of Asylum builds a just community by protecting and celebrating freedom of creative expression.

Protect

We provide sanctuary to endangered literary writers and artists, so that they can continue to create and their voices are not silenced.

Celebrate

We offer a broad range of free literary, arts, and humanities programs in a community setting to build social equity through cultural exchange.

Build Community

And by transforming abandoned properties into homes for our programs, we anchor neighborhood economic development.

Our History

22 Years of Protecting and Celebrating
Freedom of Creative Expression

A 90-second pictorial history.

Auto-advancing
1997

A Spark in the Audience

Diane Samuels and Henry Reese attend a Salman Rushdie talk at the University of Pittsburgh. He mentions the Cities of Asylum network in Europe, and an idea is born.

2004

First Writer Arrives

City of Asylum officially launches. Poet Huang Xiang paints calligraphies of his poetry on the façade of his Sampsonia Way residency house. His "House Poem" inspires our "house publication" project.

2005

First Jazz Poetry Concert

Huang Xiang collaborates with saxophonist / composer Oliver Lake, who curates and plays in Jazz Poetry for the next 15 years.

2006

A Growing Safe Haven

Winged House—a collaboration between artists Thaddeus Mosley, Laura Jean McLaughlin, Bob Ziller, and Nobel Literature Laureate Wole Soyinka—is created as the residence of our second writer. Sampsonia Way begins its transformation into a literary corridor.

2007

Our First Reading

Horacio Castellanos Moya, the second writer-in-residence, presents his novel Senselessness in the founders' home, the first of 73 programs in their house.

2009

Pittsburgh-Burma House

Text in Burmese by Khet Mar, our third writer-in-residence, and with art by her husband Than Htay Maung.

2010

Jazz House

Created by Oliver Lake and executed by Than Htay Maung. In addition to text and art, it incorporates sound.

2012

Our First Festival

"Exiled Voices of Iran," to prepare Pittsburgh for Iranian author Yaghoub Yadali, our next writer-in-residence. Finale was a concert by The Casualty Process, an Iranian electronica rock band in exile.

2013

Free Arts Programs Increase

"Summer on Sampsonia" is launched in a tent on vacant lots. It quickly becomes a community space that helps us shape our future.

2014

A River of Words

140 neighbors each mount and protect a word on their facades, honoring our protection of writers. A project of writer-in-residence Israel Centeno and Venezuelan artists Carolina Arnal and Gisela Romero.

2015

Alphabet Reading Garden

Landscape architect Joel Le Gall and artists Laura Jean McLaughlin and Diane Samuels help City of Asylum transform the site of a nuisance bar into a park for the community.

2015

International Recognition

City of Asylum Pittsburgh chosen as U.S. headquarters for the 90-city International Cities of Refuge Network.

2017

Alphabet City Opens

City of Asylum opens its permanent home at 40 W. North Avenue — Alphabet City — a cultural hub with a stage, bookstore, and restaurant.

2020

Responding to COVID

City of Asylum creates "The Show Must Go On(line) Pittsburgh," streaming our programming and hosting 21 other Pittsburgh arts organizations. We now livestream all programs.

2021

Comma House

Created by Bangladeshi writer-in-residence Tuhin Das. Installed by Thomas Herman.

2022

Ukrainian Fellowship Begins

In response to Russia's invasion of Ukraine, City of Asylum creates a special fellowship bringing three Ukrainian writers to Pittsburgh.

2024

UNESCO City of Literature

Mayor Ed Gainey selects City of Asylum to lead and organize Pittsburgh's application. U.S. withdrawal from UNESCO renders application moot.

2025

Bridges Creative Summit

City of Asylum hosts writers and leaders of the 90-city International Cities of Refuge Network in Pittsburgh.

2026

Newest Writer Arrives

Arrival of most recent endangered writer, who remains anonymous for their safety.

What Makes Us Different

A Model Like No Other

Four things that set our residency apart from other safe-haven cities.

Long-Term, Not Temporary

Other safe haven cities provide emergency relief. We commit to three years or more of protection—and helping writers build a new life in exile if they cannot return to their country safely.

Grassroots, Not Government

Our exiled writer residency program is entirely community-supported, unlike other cities in the International Cities of Refuge Network.

Every Writer Creates

Every writer has written and published while in residence—most a full-length work—and re-established their careers despite exile.

Community Integration

Writers live in the community as neighbors. They become part of the cultural fabric, through school visits, community readings, and university partnerships.

Board of Directors

Kevin McKeegan, Chair

Kevin F. McKeegan is a partner at the Pittsburgh law firm of Meyer, Unkovic & Scott LLP, where he served as Managing Partner from 2006 to 2011. He was formerly the chair of Allegheny County’s Board of Property Assessment Appeals and Review. He currently serves as vice-chair of the Allegheny County Community College Educational Foundation Board. He and his wife Sybil are the parents of three adult children and grandparents to four granddaughters.

Anne Billiet Lackner, Vice-Chair

Anne Billiet Lackner is a native of Belgium and former lawyer. She is the Honorary Consul of Belgium and co-founder of The Lackner Group, Inc., a software company for trust and estate administration. She is a member of the external advisory board at the University of Pittsburgh’s EU Center of Excellence/European Studies Center.

Barbara Talerico, Secretary

Barbara Talerico retired from BNY Mellon as Information Technology Project Manager. She is a former Certified Financial Planner with 16 years of volunteer service including Past President of Allegheny City Central Association and Mexican War Streets Society.

David Finegold, Treasurer

David Finegold is a program officer for Golub Capital’s impact philanthropy and Chief Sustainability Officer for Torrgreen. He is the founding Executive Director of National College Transfer Network and former President of Chatham University.

His awards include the Martin Luther King Memorial Prize, Guggenheim Fellowship, and James Tait Black Memorial Prize. He has been Professor of English at Yale University for over 20 years and is a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
Henry Reese is an entrepreneur and co-founder of City of Asylum. He is former principal in Reese Brothers and Communications & Commerce, former Treasurer of Cave Canem, and Board member of International Cities of Refuge Network.
Diane Samuels is a visual artist with studio and public art practices. Her permanent site-specific artworks include Luminous Manuscript, Lines of Sight, and The Alphabet Garden. She was formerly on the boards of Carnegie Museum of Art, Mattress Factory Museum, and the National Holocaust Museum at Seton Hill University. She also created Pandemic: Albert Camus, Frantz Fanon, and Rania Mamoun, a monumental artwork opposite Alphabet City on the Malta Building.
Janet Sarbaugh is retired Vice President for Creativity Programs at The Heinz Endowments. Her board service includes Chorus America, Americans for the Arts, and Grantmakers in the Arts.
Juliet Lea Hillman Simonds is Chair of The Hillman Family Foundations, Trustee of Carnegie Museums of Pittsburgh and Carnegie Mellon University, and board member of National Poetry Series and Pittsburgh Chamber Music Society.
Damon Young is a Pittsburgh author and Northside resident, winner of the Thurber Prize for American Humor, founder of VerySmartBrothas blog, contributing columnist for the Washington Post and New York Times, and inaugural writer-in-residence at the University of Pittsburgh.

Privacy Policy for City of Asylum Pittsburgh

1. Information We Collect

We only collect information that you voluntarily provide to us. For our email subscriber list, this typically includes your name and email address.

2. How We Use Your Information

We use your information strictly to send you information about programs and organization, for occasional invitations to support our mission through donations, and to respond to questions. For City of Asylum Bookstore privacy policy: cityofasylumbooks.org/privacy-policy

3. Sharing Your Information

We value your trust. We do not sell, rent, or trade our subscriber list with third parties. We only share information with trusted service providers like our email platforms, who help us send our communications and are legally bound to keep your data secure.

4. Opting Out

Most emails include an “Unsubscribe” link at the bottom. You can also contact us at contact@cityofasylumpittsburgh.org to ask us to update or delete your information from our records.

5. Security

We take reasonable steps to protect your data from unauthorized access. However, no method of online transmission is 100% secure, and providing your information is at your own risk.
